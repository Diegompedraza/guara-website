ABOUT GUARA PLATFORM

Software intelligence applied to human state in critical operations.

GUARA Platform interprets how the state of people performing critical tasks, or supervising automated systems, evolves during an operation. It turns that into capabilities that can be evaluated on existing infrastructure, explored for integration into third-party systems or deployed as a complete solution.

From isolated signals to evolving state

A person’s operational state is not adequately represented by a single frame or an isolated event. GUARA interprets how visual signals such as eye openness, blinking, gaze and head movement evolve over time, and turns that into operational information about attention, fatigue and drowsiness.

A software platform first

GUARA is not defined by a camera or a single device. The intelligence layer is the core of the company: it is designed to run locally, to be evaluated on infrastructure that organizations already operate, and to evolve toward a more modular, less hardware-dependent architecture.

Professional mobility: the first reference implementation

Professional mobility is GUARA’s first vertical. GUARA Professional, the first complete reference implementation, demonstrates the intelligence running locally on real Edge hardware. It proves the technology works; the software layer is what GUARA intends to scale.

Several ways to work with GUARA

Organizations can explore software licensing on their existing infrastructure, technology integration into their platforms, OEM / Embedded partnerships, pilots and proofs of concept, or a complete deployment. Each path starts with a technical and commercial conversation.

Beyond mobility

Logistics, mining, industry and machinery are expansion areas open to evaluation. Supervision of automated processes, teleoperation and control centers form GUARA’s long-term vision. Neither represents current customers or deployments.

Responsible validation

The platform is developed incrementally. Public descriptions distinguish demonstrated capabilities from those still under evaluation and avoid presenting preliminary evidence as broad real-world generalization.
